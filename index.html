Supporting Students' Modeling Practice Through a Large Language Model-Powered Pedagogical Agent in an Augmented Reality Learning Environment

How can students see a scientific idea?

This project brings augmented reality, scientific modeling, and AI-supported feedback into one learning environment for middle school science.

Supported by the U.S. National Science Foundation through its RITEL program.

Project focus

Observe a phenomenon.
Build a model.
Revise the explanation.

AR simulations and an on-device pedagogical agent support students as their ideas take shape.
About the project

Make the invisible parts of science easier to reason about.

Students often need to visualize movement, scale, and relationships before they can explain a scientific system. The project studies how augmented reality and timely feedback can support that work while helping teachers understand students’ evolving models.

Research objectives
AR simulation library

Explore in AR

Build simulations for middle school modeling tasks that involve complex spatial change.

Student modeling

Build and revise models

Help students create diagrammatic models that explain systems, patterns, and scientific phenomena.

On device AI

Receive useful feedback

Develop an AI agent that assesses student models and supports thoughtful revision.

Dr. Linsey Brennan is an Assistant Professor of Science Education at the University of Nevada, Reno. Her research focuses on science teacher learning, classroom discourse, and ideology through the lens of equity in science education. Dr. Brennan’s current research examines how teachers and youth collaboratively notice and respond to exclusionary practices in science classrooms. Before becoming a faculty member at the University of Nevada, Reno, Dr. Brennan taught biology, chemistry, and environmental science at an urban high school in Ohio.
